## Visualizations
A couple visualizations this week!

First, we've got the Unit Problem, illustrating Marx's argument that equating (or comparing) two non-identical things always is about equating them with respect to *something*. Marx's argument is that, insofar as markets make absolutely every product comparable with every other product (through their exchange value), and hence almost by definition abstract away from all particular use values, there is only the mere fact that they are labor products that remains. The quantitative side of labor as such (abstract labor) is value, socially necessary labor time.

(Of course, the marginal utility response would presumably be that what remains is simply "utility in the abstract", a purely quantitative sense of utility. Worth pondering what may or may not be wrong with that, but not the place for me to explore this further.)

![The Unit Problem](/assets/img/illustrations/E03_The_Unit_Problem.svg)

What kind of category is value, then? It is one that presupposes relationality (because abstract labor does not exist per se, but is the result of a real social abstraction that generalized commodity production performs).
